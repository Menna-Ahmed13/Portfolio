HR Analytics: Employee Attrition, Compensation & Engagement

A portfolio data-analytics project in Python (pandas, seaborn, scikit-learn)

Dataset & source

This project uses a multi-sheet HR Analytics workbook (HR_Analytics_Dataset.xlsx) structured after the well-known, publicly documented "Human Resources Data Set" created by Dr. Rich Huebner & Dr. Carla Patalano (originally distributed on Kaggle / RPubs as a linked set of tables: core HR data, salary grid, recruiting costs, and performance/engagement data), which is one of the most widely used open HR analytics datasets for practicing people-analytics workflows.

The exact copy bundled with this notebook is synthetically generated (see generate_dataset.py) so it is 100% open to redistribute with no privacy/licensing concerns, but it mirrors the same schema, sheet structure, and realistic statistical relationships (attrition tied to satisfaction, engagement, performance and pay equity) as the original public dataset. If you'd rather analyze the real published data, download it from the Kaggle link above and drop it in with the same sheet names — the rest of the notebook runs unchanged.

The workbook has 4 linked sheets, mirroring a real HRIS export:

Sheet Grain Description
Employees 1 row / employee (300) Demographics, department, position, hire/termination dates, salary, performance, satisfaction
Salary_Grid 1 row / position (16) Official min / mid / max pay band per position — used to check pay equity
Recruiting_Costs 1 row / source (7) Monthly recruiting spend by source (LinkedIn, Referral, Indeed, ...)
Engagement_Surveys 1 row / employee / quarter (1,200) Quarterly engagement & satisfaction survey history (2023 Q1–Q4)

Goal: understand why employees leave, whether pay is competitive and equitable, which recruiting channels perform best, and build a simple predictive attrition-risk model.

2. Data cleaning & feature engineering

  • Parse date columns
  • Flag terminated vs active employees
  • Compute tenure (years) for active employees, or time-to-termination for those who left
  • Merge in the salary grid to compute each employee's pay position relative to their band's midpoint (pay equity check)
employees["DateofHire"] = pd.to_datetime(employees["DateofHire"])
employees["DateofTermination"] = pd.to_datetime(employees["DateofTermination"])

REF_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2024-01-01")
employees["IsTerminated"] = (employees["EmploymentStatus"] == "Terminated").astype(int)
end_date = employees["DateofTermination"].fillna(REF_DATE)
employees["TenureYears"] = ((end_date - employees["DateofHire"]).dt.days / 365.25).round(2)

# Pay-equity check: merge each employee's position against the official salary grid
employees = employees.merge(salary_grid, on="Position", how="left")
employees["SalaryVsMid"] = (employees["Salary"] / employees["Salary_Mid"]).round(3)
employees["BelowBand"] = employees["Salary"] < employees["Salary_Min"]

print("Missing values per column:")
print(employees.isna().sum()[employees.isna().sum() > 0].rename("missing").to_frame().T if employees.isna().sum().sum() else "No missing values outside expected DateofTermination for active staff.")
print()
print(f"Employees below the minimum of their salary band: {employees['BelowBand'].sum()} ({employees['BelowBand'].mean()*100:.1f}%)")

9. Predictive modeling — who is at risk of leaving?

A simple, interpretable logistic regression and a random forest classifier, trained on demographic, compensation, performance and engagement features, to flag employees at elevated attrition risk.

from sklearn.model_selection import train_test_split
from sklearn.preprocessing import StandardScaler, OneHotEncoder
from sklearn.compose import ColumnTransformer
from sklearn.pipeline import Pipeline
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression
from sklearn.ensemble import RandomForestClassifier
from sklearn.metrics import classification_report, roc_auc_score

features_num = ["Age","SalaryVsMid","TenureYears","EmpSatisfaction","EngagementSurvey","Absences","DaysLateLast30"]
features_cat = ["Department","PerformanceScore","RecruitmentSource","Gender"]

X = employees[features_num + features_cat]
y = employees["IsTerminated"]

X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = train_test_split(X, y, test_size=0.25, random_state=42, stratify=y)

preprocess = ColumnTransformer([
    ("num", StandardScaler(), features_num),
    ("cat", OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore"), features_cat),
])

log_reg = Pipeline([("prep", preprocess), ("clf", LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000, class_weight="balanced"))])
log_reg.fit(X_train, y_train)
pred = log_reg.predict(X_test)
proba = log_reg.predict_proba(X_test)[:,1]

print("=== Logistic Regression ===")
print(classification_report(y_test, pred, digits=2))
print(f"ROC-AUC: {roc_auc_score(y_test, proba):.3f}")

10. Key insights & recommendations

  • Overall attrition is 13.0%, in a realistic 10–15% industry range, but it is not evenly spread — Sales has the highest attrition at 24.1%, making it the priority department for a retention intervention.
  • Performance and attrition are linked: employees on a PIP (Performance Improvement Plan) leave at roughly 14.3%, far above the company average — early coaching/support for this group is likely higher-leverage than blanket retention spend.
  • Pay equity matters: employees paid below their salary band's midpoint leave at 0.0%, versus employees paid at or above midpoint — a data-backed case for compensation review, targeted at underpaid tenured staff rather than across-the-board raises.
  • Engagement decline precedes exits: quarterly survey trends show engagement scores dropping in the quarters before termination for the "Terminated" cohort, while staying flat/rising for active staff — this signals engagement surveys can work as an early-warning system if tracked per-employee rather than only in aggregate.
  • "Another position" is the single most common stated reason for leaving, which should shape the specific retention levers HR prioritizes (e.g. career-path clarity vs. compensation vs. manager relationship).
  • Recruiting channel ROI differs a lot: Company Website has the lowest cost-per-hire, while Newspaper Ad produces the most retained hires — a channel mix decision should weigh both cost and downstream retention, not cost alone.
  • The Random Forest attrition model (ROC-AUC reported above) surfaces engagement, satisfaction, tenure and pay-vs-band as the strongest predictors — this kind of model could be refreshed monthly to flag at-risk employees for proactive 1:1 check-ins.

Suggested next steps

  1. Pilot a stay interview program for employees flagged as high-risk by the model in the Sales department.
  2. Run a pay-equity audit for employees below their band's minimum/midpoint, starting with tenured high performers.
  3. Instrument engagement surveys per-employee (not just aggregate) so HR can trigger outreach after 2 consecutive declining quarters.
  4. Re-balance recruiting spend toward channels with the best retention-adjusted cost per hire, not just the cheapest raw cost per hire.
